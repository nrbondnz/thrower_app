# Story Checkpoint: Live Quick Draw

## Story Goal
Quick draw with the phone's **live camera**: two throwers, one board each, side by side, throwing on the app's own start signal. The app times each knife from "go", sees whether it sticks and scores it, and says who won, readable from the throwing line. Nigel, 2026-10-15: *"start the live camera quick draw, but as a different button so its clear, one version is for scoring rounds on one target and the other is quick draw."* Builds on the recording version, [[story-checkpoint-quick-draw]] (archived). Backlog #9 (and the live-camera part of #10).

**Not in this story:** recording and sharing with scoring overlays, for Quick draw and for Play. That's the next story ("Record and share", [[backlog]] #11), with Nigel's decisions already taken (below).

## Complexity Score
7 (Large): UI → camera → vision → game (4), a new start-signal flow with false starts (1), platform-dependent camera settings (60 fps, sideways Android frames) (1), more than 3 acceptance criteria (1).

## Decisions (Nigel, 2026-10-15)
- **A separate Home button, "Quick draw"**, beside **Play**: Play stays single-target rounds (9 rounds of 3); Quick draw is the two-thrower duel. Live test → Quick draw stays for the recording.
- **Two stories**, live Quick draw first, then record and share.
- For the next story (recorded here so it isn't lost): overlays are **composited after recording** (the camera's own video plus a timeline of scoring events, drawn on by the phone's video framework when sharing: AVFoundation on iOS, Media3 on Android; no FFmpeg); **Play records each throw as a short clip**; recordings have **sound from the microphone** (a new permission: Review Agent).

## Carried Over (decided earlier)
- The winner: the **first knife to stick** wins; a bounce-out or a false start loses; the same frame is a draw; none stuck, no winner (2026-10-14).
- The start signal: **like Formula 1, 5 beeps, a random pause, then a longer "go" beep** (Nigel and Mark, 2026-10-14). The beeps play with the iPhone's silent switch on and duck other audio.
- Boards are chosen by **tapping each centre, Player 1 then Player 2**, then locked; there may be more boards in view (2026-10-14).
- **Analyse every frame in order, report late**; analysis starts **1 s before go** (2026-10-15). Any earlier is an obvious false start.
- Impact = the frame with the largest change on the board's face (`MotionEpisode.impact`).
- Safety (Review Agent, Quick draw Safety Report): the in-app instructions' Quick draw points; **the result must be readable from the throwing line**; nobody needs to touch the phone between "Play" and the result.

## Acceptance Criteria
1. Home has **Play** (rounds on one target) and **Quick draw** (two throwers) as separate buttons, each saying what it is.
2. Quick draw opens the live camera full screen; tapping Player 1's board, then Player 2's, outlines and numbers them; Lock boards.
3. Start: 5 beeps, a random pause, a long, higher "go" beep as the lights go out (no GO! on screen: Nigel, 2026-10-16); heard with the silent switch on.
4. Every camera frame from 1 s before go is analysed, in order (the result may come late); each knife is timed from go and judged stuck (with its score) or bounced; a knife before go is a false start.
5. The result is shown **large enough to read from the throwing line** (about 4 m), with the winner and each player's time and score.
6. **Next duel** (tapped once the knives are collected) keeps the boards locked. (Reworded 2026-10-15 to match what was built: a tap, not detecting the collection.)
7. Works with the phone upright on both platforms (Android's sideways frames, iOS's upright ones). The recording version (Live test → Quick draw) and Play are unchanged.

## Task 2: 60 fps, and every frame's board crops in order
- **Status:** BUILT (2026-10-15); device check by Nigel's brother pending (Device Test Plan Q2, Q4, Q7), assumed OK to go on (Nigel: "Can we make more progress assuming he would say its all good").
- **What changed:**
  - `LiveCamera.open(fps:)`: Quick draw's camera (`liveQuickDrawCameraProvider`) asks for **60**; if the phone won't open at it (a camera error that isn't a refused permission), it opens again at its default. Play and Calibrate are unchanged.
  - `CroppedCamera` handles **rotation**: the upright rectangle is mapped into the sensor's layout (`sensorRect`) and cut there; the crop reports the source's rotation, so whoever turns it upright gets exactly the rectangle. No whole frame is ever turned. All four sides rounded to even (YUV colour planes).
  - `compactFrame`: a tight copy of a cropped frame (it otherwise keeps the whole source frame alive).
  - `QueuedCamera` (`lib/camera/queued_camera.dart`): every frame's crop is kept as a compact copy and handed on in order, one per turn of the event loop; `waiting`, `behind`, and `dropped` past **75 MB** a board (a memory guard; was 600 frames, see the Safety Report).
  - `QuickDrawWatcher(queue: true)` for the live camera; `states` and `queues` for showing progress.
  - Live screen: **Lock boards** starts watching both boards; debug builds show "1: watching · 2: motion · 0.03 s behind" above the instruction line. Start again stops it.
- **Safety Report (2026-10-15): OK for local testing**, after two fixes:
  - ✅ No manifest, plist or pubspec change. The 60 fps fallback never hides a refused permission. Play and Calibrate are unchanged. The debug line is `kDebugMode` only. Crops stay in memory.
  - ⚠️→✅ **Memory:** a 600-frame cap could reach 1–2.5 GB with large crops on a phone that never catches up (watching runs from Lock until Start again). **Fixed:** the cap is in bytes, 75 MB a board (1.2–6 s at 60 fps, by crop size). Task 3 also keeps a full backlog only from 1 s before go.
  - ⚠️→✅ **Rotation:** read on every frame, so a changed device orientation could cut the wrong region or crash. **Fixed:** `CroppedCamera` keeps the rotation from when it's made (Lock), and skips a frame its rectangle doesn't fit.
  - ⚠️ **60 fps is applied silently**: iOS picks the nearest rate at 720p, Android's CameraX does its best (it may stay at 30, or change the stream size); the fallback only covers errors. A fixed 60 fps caps exposure at 1/60 s: darker, noisier frames in dim light. Check on both phones in the dimmest lighting expected; the debug frames/s label (top left) shows what each delivers.
  - ⚠️ **Heat and stutter:** cropping, brightness and motion work run on the UI thread at 60 fps × 2 boards while locked. Run 10+ minutes on the slower phone watching "s behind" and the temperature; a long-lived worker isolate is the fix if needed.

  **Gaps found:**
  1. **Likely bug, the start signal may be silent on a real phone.** `signalSoundsProvider` is `Provider.autoDispose`, but `LiveQuickDrawScreen` only `ref.read`s it (in `_start` and `_checkResult`) and nothing watches it. Checked with Riverpod 3.4.3 (a scratch `ProviderContainer` probe): an auto-dispose provider that is only read is disposed straight after the read, and the next read makes a new one. So the `AssetSignalSounds` given to `StartSignal` has its players disposed (`ref.onDispose(sounds.dispose)`) as the countdown starts, and the result chime comes from a fresh instance that hasn't loaded its sounds yet. Widget tests can't see this because they use `overrideWithValue(FakeSignalSounds)`. Fix (a code change, for Nigel to approve): `ref.watch(signalSoundsProvider)` in `build` (this also preloads the sounds when the screen opens), or drop `autoDispose`. Add a widget test that keeps the real provider shape (override with `overrideWith((ref) { … ref.onDispose(…) })`) and asserts it isn't disposed during a duel. **Must be fixed before the brother's Q3.**
  2. **No test for the 60 fps fallback or `AssetSignalSounds`** (plugin code, like `AssetRecordingSound`). Accepted: device checks Q3 and Q7.
  3. **The live screen's result panel is only tested with "No winner".** No widget test drives a stuck/bounced/false-start throw through `LiveQuickDrawScreen` (the "both throws in" result path, the winner headline, the per-player lines). The logic is covered by `quick_draw_judge_test` and `quick_draw_live_path_test`. Optional: a screen test fed the live-path frames on a fake camera.
  4. **Stale criterion wording.** C6 says "without touching the board calibration (after the knives are collected)": as built, **Next duel** is a tap and doesn't detect collection (Plan Changes Log, Task 4). Suggested: "Next duel (tapped once the knives are collected) keeps the boards locked." C2's plan text (Task 1 Work) still says "with zoom as on Calibrate": dropped (Plan Changes Log, Task 1). Task 4's Save says the live path is tested "sideways and upright": only sideways is (upright is covered through the recording by `quick_draw_replay_test`, not through `QueuedCamera`).
  5. **C4 "every frame" has one exception:** past 75 MB a board, `QueuedCamera` drops the oldest frames. `dropped` shows only in the debug line (`kDebugMode`), so a release build would give a result from incomplete frames without saying so. Consider showing it in the result, or note it in Known Issues.

  **Orphan / backward check:**
  - `TargetCamera.now`: used by `LiveQuickDrawScreen` (the start signal's clock, the result window). `RecordedCamera.now` is used in screen tests (`clockCamera`). `CroppedCamera.now` and `QueuedCamera.now` are pass-throughs for the interface, and nothing calls them. `RecordingAnalysisFeed.now` (the playback position) isn't used: the recording's `QuickDrawScreen` gets go from the recording's metadata. No test covers any of the three. Not orphans (the interface needs them), but untested.
